Florida UV is the dominant stressor for headlight lenses — the year-round intensity here destroys the factory UV-protective coating on polycarbonate faster than almost any other climate in the country. Leesburg adds to that: the heavy oak canopy along Silver Lake and residential streets off Dixie Avenue deposits pollen and organic residue onto lenses regularly, and the elevated humidity from Lake Harris and Lake Griffin means that residue bonds to the polycarbonate surface faster than it would in a drier environment. Vehicles parked near Venetian Gardens or in driveways off North 14th Street deal with both stressors simultaneously.

Once the factory coating fails, the polycarbonate oxidizes and turns yellow, hazy, or milky. Headlight polishing removes the damaged outer layer completely, restores the lens to optical clarity, and applies a new UV-resistant sealant to protect it going forward. What's Included

✓
Lens Assessment

Each lens examined before starting to determine oxidation depth and the correct sanding progression. Heavily damaged lenses require more stages to restore properly.

✓
Masking

Paint and trim around each lens masked before sanding begins to protect surrounding surfaces from accidental abrasion during the wet sanding stages.

✓
Wet Sanding

Multi-stage wet sanding removes the oxidized outer layer of the lens completely. Typically progresses through 3 to 5 grits. This is the stage most DIY kits skip or rush, which is why their results do not last.

✓
Machine Polishing

Dual-action polisher with cutting compound removes sanding marks and restores optical clarity. Finishing polish applied for maximum transparency before the sealant goes on.

✓
UV Sealant Application

Professional UV-resistant coating applied to the polished lens. Replaces the factory protection that failed and creates a barrier against both UV and the organic residue the Leesburg oak canopy and lake humidity deposit on lenses over time.

✓
Final Inspection

Lenses inspected in direct light and at angle to confirm full clarity before the job is called complete.

Our Process

1

Assessment and Masking

Each lens examined to determine oxidation severity and sanding plan. Paint and trim around the lens masked to protect against abrasion before any work begins.

2

Coarse Wet Sand

Starting grit selected for the level of oxidation present. Lens wet sanded in uniform passes to remove the damaged outer layer completely. The lens looks worse at this stage — that is correct. All oxidation has to come off before clarity can be restored.

3

Progressive Sanding

Progressively finer grits refine the surface and remove scratches left by the previous stage. Each pass prepares the lens for the next. Skipping grits leaves scratches that polishing cannot fully remove.

4

Machine Polish

Dual-action polisher with cutting compound removes the finest sanding marks and brings the lens back to full transparency. Finishing polish applied for maximum clarity.

5

UV Sealant

Professional UV-resistant coating applied and allowed to cure. Replaces the factory protection that broke down and protects the polished lens against both UV and the canopy and lake-humidity residue specific to the Leesburg environment.
