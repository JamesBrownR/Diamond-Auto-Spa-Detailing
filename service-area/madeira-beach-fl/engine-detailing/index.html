Coastal vehicles kept near the Gulf of Mexico develop engine bay buildup with a character specific to that environment. Salt air works into the engine bay continuously and deposits chloride residue onto every surface, including rubber hoses, plastic covers, metal brackets, and wiring. That salt residue mixes with the oil and fluid buildup that accumulates through normal use and with road film from coastal driving. The result is stickier, more corrosive, and more damaging to metal components and rubber over time than the equivalent level of buildup on an inland vehicle. Salt accelerates corrosion on fasteners and brackets that would otherwise last for years without attention.

Engine detailing removes all of it safely. Sensitive electrical components are protected before any water is introduced. Degreaser penetrates and lifts the combined salt and grease buildup with proper dwell time. Dedicated brushes work through every accessible area. A controlled low-pressure rinse clears the dissolved contamination. Dressing applied to all rubber and plastic surfaces afterward helps slow the ongoing salt and UV deterioration that coastal vehicles face under the hood year-round.

What's Included

Electrical Protection

✓
Degreaser Application and Dwell

Professional-grade degreaser applied across all surfaces and allowed to dwell. Coastal salt and grease deposits require proper penetration time before agitation can fully release them.

✓
Brush Agitation

Multiple dedicated brushes worked through the entire bay. Firewall, strut towers, battery tray, around the engine block, along hose and wiring runs, and into every accessible recess.

✓
Low-Pressure Rinse

Controlled low-pressure rinse removes dissolved grease, salt deposits, and degreaser residue without forcing water into sensitive areas. Not a pressure washer blast. Controlled and methodical throughout.

✓
Dry Down

Compressed air removes standing water from tight areas, wiring channels, and component gaps. Bay allowed to fully dry before any dressing is applied.

✓
Dressing and Protection

All plastic covers, rubber hoses, and trim pieces dressed with a non-greasy protectant. Slows the salt-driven deterioration and UV degradation that accelerates rubber cracking and corrosion on coastal vehicles faster than anywhere inland.

Our Process

1

Pre-Clean Assessment

Andrew opens the hood and evaluates the level of buildup, identifies any visible corrosion, leaks, or concerns, and covers all sensitive electrical components and intakes before starting. Any concerns flagged to the customer before work begins.

2

Degreaser Application and Dwell

Degreaser applied across all surfaces. Coastal vehicles with salt residue mixed into the grease layer get proper dwell time to ensure full penetration. Rushing this step leaves the salt-contaminated residue behind.

3

Agitation

Dedicated brushes worked through the entire bay. Firewall, strut towers, battery tray area, around the engine block, along hose and wiring runs, and into every accessible recess. The goal is to lift every accessible deposit before rinsing.

4

Rinse

Controlled low-pressure rinse removes dissolved contamination and degreaser. Water directed away from sensitive components. A second degreaser pass applied to any areas that did not fully clean on the first round.

5

Dry and Dress

Compressed air removes standing water from tight areas. Bay dried thoroughly before dressing begins. Protectant applied to all plastic and rubber surfaces. Final inspection to confirm everything is clean and all protective coverings removed. Any developing corrosion or wear flagged to the owner.
