Engine bays on Umatilla vehicles accumulate a specific combination of contamination that builds harder than in most suburban areas. Oil residue, road dust from unpaved county roads along SR-19 and CR-450, and fine citrus pollen from the surrounding grove corridor all settle onto engine bay surfaces and get baked on by Florida heat during every drive. The consistent high temperatures under the hood in this climate bond deposits to plastic covers, rubber hoses, and painted metal surfaces faster than in cooler states. A bay that looks moderately dirty often has layers of contamination that are set significantly harder than they appear.

Beyond cleanliness, a clean engine bay makes it easier to spot developing leaks, cracks, or wear on hoses and belts before they become bigger problems. On trucks and work vehicles used for agricultural activity around Umatilla, keeping the bay maintained also protects the rubber and plastic components from the accelerated degradation that heat, humidity, and agricultural dust drive in this environment. The dressing applied after cleaning slows that process and keeps components in better condition between services.

What Is Included

✓
Pre-Clean Assessment and Component Protection

Andrew opens the hood, evaluates the level and type of buildup, checks for any visible leaks or concerns, and covers all sensitive electrical components and intakes before any product is applied. Any concerns flagged to the customer before work begins.

✓
Degreaser Application and Dwell

Degreaser applied across all surfaces. Umatilla vehicles with heat-baked agricultural dust and oil residue from rural driving get longer dwell time to ensure the product fully breaks down the bonded deposits before agitation begins. Florida heat bakes contamination on harder than in cooler states, and dwell time is the variable that determines whether the agitation step can actually move it.

✓
Brush Agitation

Multiple dedicated brushes worked through the entire bay. Firewall, strut towers, battery tray, around the engine block, along hose and wiring runs, and into every accessible recess. The goal is to lift every deposit that the degreaser has broken loose before rinsing begins.

✓
Low-Pressure Rinse

Controlled low-pressure rinse removes dissolved grease, agricultural dust, and degreaser residue without forcing water into sensitive areas. Not a pressure washer blast. Controlled and methodical throughout. A second degreaser pass applied to any areas that did not fully clean on the first round.

✓
Dry Down

Compressed air removes standing water from tight areas, wiring channels, and component gaps. Bay allowed to fully dry before any dressing is applied.

✓
Dressing and Protection

All plastic covers, rubber hoses, and trim pieces dressed with a non-greasy protectant. Restores color and slows the Florida heat and humidity degradation that dries out and cracks rubber components faster under the hood in this climate, particularly on work trucks and agricultural vehicles used regularly around Umatilla.

Our Process

1

Pre-Clean Assessment

Andrew opens the hood and evaluates the level of buildup, identifies any visible leaks or concerns, and covers all sensitive electrical components and intakes before starting. Any concerns flagged to the customer before work begins.

2

Degreaser Application and Dwell

Degreaser applied across all surfaces. Agricultural dust and oil residue from SR-19 and CR-450 rural driving get appropriate dwell time to fully break down before agitation. Rushing this step leaves bonded deposits behind that agitation alone cannot remove.

3

Agitation

Dedicated brushes worked through the entire bay. Firewall, strut towers, battery tray area, around the engine block, along hose and wiring runs, and into every accessible recess. The goal is to lift every accessible deposit before rinsing.

4

Rinse

Controlled low-pressure rinse removes dissolved contamination and degreaser. Water directed away from sensitive components. A second degreaser pass applied to any areas needing additional attention before a final rinse.

5

Dry and Dress

Compressed air removes standing water from tight areas and wiring channels. Bay dried thoroughly before dressing begins. Protectant applied to all plastic and rubber surfaces. Final inspection to confirm everything is clean and all protective coverings removed before closing the hood.
