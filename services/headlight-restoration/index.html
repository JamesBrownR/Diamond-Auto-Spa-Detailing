Why Headlights Go Yellow in Florida

Modern headlight lenses are made from polycarbonate plastic, not glass. The factory applies a thin UV-protective coating to the surface when the vehicle is new. In most climates that coating holds up reasonably well. In Florida it does not. The combination of intense UV radiation year-round, heat, humidity, and salt air in coastal areas breaks down the coating within a few years. Once the coating fails, UV attacks the polycarbonate directly and oxidation sets in fast.

The result is the yellowed, hazy, or cloudy lenses you see on most vehicles that have spent more than three or four years in Central Florida. Beyond the cosmetic issue, heavily oxidized lenses scatter and block light output from the bulb behind them. Studies have shown that severely degraded lenses reduce usable road illumination by over 80 percent. That is a real safety problem, not just an appearance concern.

Headlight restoration removes the oxidized and damaged outer layer of the lens, polishes the exposed plastic back to clarity, and applies a new UV-resistant sealant to protect it going forward. The whole process is performed mobile. Andrew comes to your location with everything needed.

Our Process

1

Lens Assessment and Masking

Each lens examined to determine oxidation severity and sanding plan. Paint and trim around the lens masked off to protect against sanding damage. Takes a few minutes but prevents costly mistakes.

2

Coarse Wet Sand

Starting with the appropriate grit for the level of oxidation, the lens is wet sanded in uniform passes to remove the damaged outer layer completely. The lens looks worse at this stage, which is correct. All the oxidation and hazing has to come off before clarity can be restored.

3

Progressive Sanding

Progressively finer grits used in sequence to refine the surface and remove scratches left by the previous stage. Each pass prepares the lens for the next. Skipping grits produces visible scratches that polishing cannot fully remove.

4

Machine Polish

Dual-action polisher with cutting compound removes the finest sanding marks and brings the lens back to transparency. Finishing polish applied for maximum clarity. At this point the lens looks new.

5

UV Sealant

Professional UV-resistant coating applied and allowed to cure. This replaces the factory coating that broke down over time and is what makes the restoration last. Without this step, the bare polycarbonate begins oxidizing again almost immediately in Florida sun.

Restoration vs. Replacement

"My other SUV he brought the headlights back to life. They are crystal clear again. Honestly I cannot recommend him enough." - Jonathan Wilson

Replacement headlight assemblies for most vehicles run between $150 and $500 per side at a parts counter, and significantly more for European or luxury vehicles. Add labor at a shop and you are looking at a four-figure repair on a vehicle where the underlying bulb and housing are still perfectly functional. The only thing wrong is the outer lens surface.

Restoration addresses exactly that. The process removes the damaged surface layer, restores clarity, and protects the fresh polycarbonate so it holds up going forward. On most vehicles the result is visually indistinguishable from a new lens. For the cases where it is not quite perfect, it is still dramatically better than before and costs a fraction of what replacement would run.

The one situation where replacement genuinely makes more sense is internal fogging or moisture intrusion inside the housing. That cannot be fixed from the outside and indicates a seal failure. If Andrew sees that during the assessment he will tell you before starting work.
