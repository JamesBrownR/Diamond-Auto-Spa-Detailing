What Is Paint Correction?

Paint correction is the process of using machine polishers, cutting compounds, and progressively finer polishes to remove defects from the clear coat. Unlike a standard detail that applies wax or sealant on top of whatever the paint looks like, paint correction actually removes the defects. Swirl marks, light scratches, water etching, and oxidation are physically abraded away, leveling the clear coat surface and restoring it to a flat, mirror-like finish.

Most paint defects are in the clear coat, which sits as the outermost layer on top of the color base coat. The clear coat is typically 50 to 100 microns thick. Machine polishing removes a small fraction of that depth to eliminate surface-level defects. Done correctly with proper thickness measurements and technique, it can be performed multiple times over a vehicle's life without compromising the clear coat.

The results under direct sunlight are the real test. Paint that looks acceptable in shade or overcast light often reveals heavy swirling, hazing, and water etching the moment the sun hits it at the right angle. After correction, that same paint shows a deep, clear reflection with no scatter. That is the difference.

What's Included

✓
Full Decontamination Wash

Hand wash, iron remover, and clay bar before any polishing begins. Bonded surface contamination must be fully removed before correction work. Polishing over contaminated paint causes new scratches.

✓
Paint Thickness Measurement

Clear coat thickness measured across all panels before starting. Identifies areas with reduced clear coat from previous polishing, body repair, or factory thin spots. Guides how aggressively each area can be worked safely.

✓
Multi-Stage Machine Polishing

Dual-action or rotary polisher used with cutting compound on heavier defects, followed by progressively finer polish stages to refine the finish. Each stage removes the marks left by the previous one. The number of stages depends on paint condition.

✓
Panel-by-Panel Work

Correction performed one panel at a time with the machine set to the correct speed and pressure for that panel. Flat panels, curved edges, and tight areas all require different technique. Andrew adjusts throughout rather than running a single pass across the whole car.

✓
Final Inspection Under Lighting

Completed paint inspected under dedicated lighting at multiple angles before the job is called done. Any remaining defects addressed before finishing. The car is not done until it passes inspection.

✓
Paint Protection Application

Corrected paint finished with a paint sealant or wax to protect the fresh clear coat surface. If ceramic coating is being applied, IPA wipe-down is performed instead and coating follows.

Correction Stages Explained

1

Single-Stage Correction

One pass with a cutting compound and appropriate pad removes light swirl marks, minor water spots, and light oxidation. Suitable for vehicles in reasonably good condition that have accumulated normal wear. Results are significant but not as thorough as multi-stage work. Typically used as prep for ceramic coating on well-maintained vehicles.

2

Two-Stage Correction

First stage uses a heavier cutting compound to address deeper swirl marks, water etching, and moderate oxidation. Second stage refines the finish with a finishing polish to remove any haze or micro-marring left by the cut. Most vehicles benefit from two-stage correction. The jump in finish quality over a single stage is considerable.

3

Multi-Stage Correction

Three or more polishing stages for heavily defected paint, severe oxidation, deep water etching, or paint that has been neglected for many years. Andrew has spent an entire day on a single correction job because the paint required it. This is the level of work that produces show-quality results on vehicles that looked beyond saving.

What Paint Correction Can and Cannot Fix

"One was pretty oxidized and when he was done the deep reds were back. The trim all looked brand new, the interior looked stunning as well. Honestly I cannot recommend him enough." - Jonathan Wilson

Can be corrected with polishing: swirl marks from automatic car washes and improper washing, light to moderate scratches within the clear coat, water etching and hard water spots, light oxidation and hazing, buffer trails from previous detailing work, and most paint contamination that survived decontamination.

Cannot be corrected with polishing alone: scratches that have cut through the clear coat into the base coat or primer, stone chips and deep gouges, rust, paint chips where the color coat is missing, and clear coat that has already failed and is peeling. These require touch-up paint, spot respray, or panel respray depending on severity. If Andrew sees these during assessment he will tell you before starting.

The honest answer on any given vehicle is somewhere on that spectrum. Most vehicles have a mix of correctable and non-correctable defects. The goal of the assessment is to set accurate expectations so there are no surprises when the job is done.
